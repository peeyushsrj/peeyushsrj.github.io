Slowing down inside a fast-paced tech job is less about doing less and more about creating texture in your day - moments where your brain isn't being pulled toward the next thing. Since you're not going outside in the morning or evening, the work has to happen indoors, which actually makes it more deliberate.

A few honest thoughts:

Stop treating recovery like another task. The trap is reading "10 tips for slow living," adding meditation to your calendar, optimizing your tea ritual, and now slowness itself has a backlog. Slow living isn't a productivity system. It's the absence of one in certain hours.

Create a hard boundary between work and not-work. When you're done with the day's tickets, actually be done. Close the terminal. Put the laptop in another room if you can. The DevOps brain loves to keep half-monitoring things - Slack, alerts, "let me just check one thing." That bleeding is what makes life feel fast even when you're sitting still.

Single-tasking is the whole game. Eat without a screen. Drink chai while drinking chai, not while scrolling. This sounds trite but it's the actual mechanism. Fast-paced life isn't about speed, it's about constantly splitting attention. One thing at a time, even boring things, slows time down.

Pick one slow hobby that has no output. Not something you'll post about, optimize, or get better at on a measurable curve. Reading fiction, cooking something that takes hours, sketching badly, learning an instrument privately, gardening on a balcony, journaling by hand. The point is doing something where the doing is the point.

Since you're home-bound morning and evening - can I gently ask why? If it's preference, no problem, design around it. If it's something heavier (low energy, low mood, social anxiety, just stuck in a rut), that's worth naming to yourself, because being indoors 24/7 can quietly compound into something that feels like "fast life burnout" but is actually closer to isolation. Even opening a window in the morning, sitting on a balcony or near it with chai for 10 minutes, watching the street wake up - that counts as outside-ish and your nervous system registers it.

Practical things that actually help DevOps folks specifically:

Mute non-critical Slack channels ruthlessly. Turn off PR notification emails. Batch your "check things" moments instead of letting them run all day. Take a real lunch away from the desk. After your last task, do something with your hands for 30 minutes before touching your phone - cook, tidy, stretch. The transition ritual matters more than the evening itself.
