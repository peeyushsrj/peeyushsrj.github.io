The mind-as-garden analogy works well for your situation. Let me walk through it.

The core idea: Your mind is a garden. Thoughts, worries, and tasks are seeds. Whatever you water grows. Whatever you neglect, withers. You are the gardener — not every seed deserves your water.

## Weeds vs. Crops

Right now, "I'm blocked waiting on Alice" and "there's so much to do" are probably running loops in your head. Those are weeds — they consume mental nutrients without producing anything. The actual crops are the few tasks you can move forward today. A good gardener spends most of their time on crops, not pulling the same weed over and over.

## You Can't Rush Growth

A teammate's PR review, an approval, someone else's deploy — these are seeds planted in their garden. You watered them when you sent the request. Checking on them every hour is like digging up a seed to see if it's sprouted. Plant it, note when to check back, move to another bed.

## Seasonal Thinking

Not everything blooms at once. As a devops engineer you know this intuitively — you don't run every pipeline simultaneously, you stage them. Your work has seasons too:

- Some weeks you're **planting** (kicking off dependencies, writing tickets)
- Some you're **tending** (reviewing, debugging)
- Some you're **harvesting** (shipping)

Trying to do all three at peak intensity is how gardens — and engineers — burn out.

## Fences and Beds

A garden has structure. Beds for tomatoes, beds for herbs. Without that, everything tangles. For you, that means:

- A "waiting on others" bed (visible but not actively worked)
- A "today" bed (2-3 things, max)
- A "later" bed (parked, not forgotten)

The mistake is treating everything as one chaotic plot.

## The Practical Move

When you notice yourself spinning on blocked work, ask: "Is this a crop I'm tending or a weed I'm watering?" If it's blocked on someone else, you've already watered it. Walk to a different bed.

The analogy's real gift is permission — permission to not carry every task with equal weight, and to accept that some things grow on a timeline that isn't yours.
